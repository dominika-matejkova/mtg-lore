Avacynprotector of Innistrad

Avacyn was an archangel forged by the vampire planeswalker Sorin Markov to protect Innistrad’s humanity in his absence. She rose to become the central figure of the Church of Avacyn — then fell to corruption and was unmade by her own creator. Three cards show her: Avacyn, Guardian Angel (2014), Archangel Avacyn // Avacyn, the Purifier (2016), and Avacyn, Angel of Hope (2012).

Who she is

The MTG Wiki describes Avacyn as “an archangel who inhabited Innistrad” and “the central figure of the Church of Avacyn, who gave the humanity of that plane the faith to fight for survival.” She was not born but made: Sorin Markov created her and invested her with the power to maintain the balance between humanity and the plane’s darker forces.

Origin
Created on Innistrad by the vampire planeswalker Sorin Markov, who thought of her as a kind of daughter and tasked her with protecting the plane in his long absences.
Purpose
To maintain the balance between humans, vampires, werewolves, and demons — without her, the Wiki says, humanity’s extinction at the hands of the supernatural was inevitable, which would in turn doom the vampires and werewolves that fed on them.
Psychology
Before her corruption, incredibly benevolent toward humans and most comfortable with children. She didn’t understand smiling, but felt deep satisfaction in her work. Sigarda called her the most powerful and confident angel on Innistrad, if also the most detached — a consequence, perhaps, of her artificial nature.
Capacity for violence
Not limited to her corrupted state: a thousand years before the events on this page, she single-handedly slew the angel Liesa and her entire flight for allying with a demon lord, then forbade anyone from speaking Liesa’s name again.
Status
Deceased. Unmade by her own creator, Sorin Markov, at the Vault of the Archangel where she was first made.

Her story

Avacyn’s arc runs from her creation through a long disappearance, a joyful return, a slow corruption she never saw coming, and a death that broke the last of Sorin’s protections over Innistrad.

  1. Created to protect Avacyn

    As Innistrad’s vampires gain ground against humanity, Sorin Markov forges Avacyn and tasks her with holding back the darkness. The Church of Avacyn rises from the power he invests in her, becoming humanity’s central source of faith and defense.

    MTG Wiki: Avacyn

  2. Trapped in the Helvault Avacyn

    The demon Griselbrand challenges Avacyn to combat at the Helvault, and the two battle for days before Avacyn drives him into the vault with the last of her strength. As his final act, Griselbrand impales her with his spear; the binding spell backfires, and Avacyn is imprisoned in the Helvault alongside every demon trapped within. Her disappearance shatters the balance she was built to maintain.

    MTG Wiki: Avacyn

  3. The Sundering of the Helvault Avacyn

    Liliana Vess, seeking to destroy Griselbrand and free herself from a demonic pact, forces Thalia to open the Helvault. Both the demons and Avacyn are released. Restored, Avacyn leads Innistrad’s humanity into roughly a year of prosperity, and transforms a group of lycanthropes at Gatstaf Grotto into a new race, the wolfir, who pledge to guard humanity in exchange for relief from their curse — a wave of magic known as the Cursemute.

    MTG Wiki: Avacyn

  4. The first cracks Avacyn Corruption

    Rescuing a village outcast’s son from the woods, Avacyn is struck by a strange spell — unknown to her, the backlash of Nahiri’s cryptoliths summoning Emrakul to the plane, which is especially harmful to angels, beings of raw mana. The spell floods her mind with visions of Nahiri’s works and a new conviction that humanity itself is corrupted. Black trim begins to creep into her irises.

    MTG Wiki: Avacyn

  5. Full corruption Corruption

    Avacyn’s madness deepens: she verbally abuses a Lunarch who questions her, kills a grieving werewolf who prays to her, and threatens her own angelic sister Sigarda. By late Hunter’s Moon she commands a flight of angels in slaughtering the same village she once protected, hearing “soothing whispers from the Moon” as she attacks.

    MTG Wiki: Avacyn

  6. The Unmaking Avacyn Corruption

    At Thraben Cathedral, a mad Avacyn attacks Jace Beleren and Tamiyo before Sorin arrives and reveals himself as her creator. In a moment of lucidity she recognizes him — then denounces him as the one responsible for the flaw that doomed her. They fight through the cathedral; Sorin offers to heal her, but she refuses, declaring “I am no monster’s instrument. I will not be altered by the likes of you.” Rather than bind her, Sorin unravels the spell that formed her. Her last words: “I am Avacyn. I am to protect.” Her faithful, lacking marble, built her memorial from wood, straw, twine, and clay.

    MTG Wiki: Avacyn

This timeline rests on one primary source, the MTG Wiki’s Avacyn page, itself built from citations to the original Wizards of the Coast story chapters.

Playing her as a commander

Avacyn, Angel of Hope is her most played build, leading 4,373 decks on EDHREC — an angels tribal theme (282 decks) backed by land destruction (114) and lifegain (107), leaning on her blanket indestructible to protect a board while destroying the opponents’ lands and permanents. Archangel Avacyn leads a smaller 1,529 decks, more aggressive and equipment-focused (blink 71 decks, burn 71, equipment 43, voltron 35). Avacyn, Guardian Angel is a niche build at only 96 decks, mostly pillow-fort and control strategies built around her damage-prevention abilities. EDHREC counts decks of every pod size, not 1v1 specifically.

What could not be confirmed

  • Whether she stays dead. I found no source suggesting a return for Avacyn after the Unmaking; the MTG Wiki lists her death plainly in its infobox, so her page here treats her as deceased.
  • The Echoverse variant. An alternate-universe Avacyn with inverted colors (black hair and wings, white clothes) appears in the 2026 Reality Fracture story; I treated this as non-canon to her main arc and left it out of the timeline above.
  • Exact wording of the story chapters. I read the MTG Wiki through a page-reading tool rather than the original Wizards story articles it cites, so check direct quotes against the source stories before relying on exact phrasing.
