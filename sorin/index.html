Sorin Markovthe Mortifier

Sorin Markov is a vampire planeswalker and master of sangromancy, one of the oldest living planeswalkers at over 7,000 years old. Eight cards and one transforming creature show him under his own name, spanning 2011 to 2024, from his debut as a self-interested wanderer to the creator and eventual destroyer of the angel Avacyn.

Who he is

The MTG Wiki describes Sorin as a 6’2”-tall, pale-skinned vampire with white hair and eyes with black sclera and gold irises. He is a master of sangromancy — blood magic capable of draining lifeforce, reducing creatures to ash, cursing enemies, possessing minds, and shaping blood into weapons. He is naturally capable of short flight and moves in near-total silence.

Age
Born c. −2455 AR in Stensia, Innistrad — more than 7,000 years old at the time of the Siege of Thraben, and one of only a few surviving pre-Mending planeswalkers.
Family
Grandson of Edgar Markov. He regards his creation, the archangel Avacyn, as a kind of daughter, and has sired many vampires of his own.
Temperament
A bon vivant who has dedicated his long existence to following his whims, giving him a detachment and easy confidence that other ancient planeswalkers like Ugin and Nicol Bolas lack.
Signature weapon
The Parasite Blade, a two-handed sword enchanted to feed off the mana of any creature it cuts, capable of reducing even large creatures to ash. Olivia Voldaren took it from him after Nahiri defeated him.
Status
Alive. As of the March of the Machine era, he works alongside Arlinn Kord to prepare Innistrad’s defenses against the Phyrexian multiverse invasion.

His story

Sorin’s is the longest story of any character in this archive — millennia spent protecting Innistrad from a distance, the creation and eventual destruction of his own angel, and a centuries-long feud with the lithomancer Nahiri that nearly cost him everything. This timeline covers the major beats; several side episodes (his dealings with Dack Fayden, the two conflicting Tarkir timelines, non-canon Boom! comics material) are compressed or omitted for length.

  1. A forced ascension Sorin

    At 16, Sorin is held down by his own family and forced to drink the blood of the archangel Marycz as part of Shilgengar’s Ritual — the same dark blood magic his grandfather Edgar Markov used to found the Innistrad vampires. The trauma of the transformation ignites Sorin’s planeswalker spark.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  2. Binding the Eldrazi Sorin Nahiri

    About a thousand years after his ascension, Sorin allies with the Kor lithomancer Nahiri and the spirit dragon Ugin to trap the world-devouring Eldrazi on the plane of Zendikar, using a hedron matrix to force the titans into physical, imprisonable form. The three planeswalkers agree to return together if the prison is ever disturbed, then go their separate ways.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  3. Creating Avacyn Sorin

    Watching from afar as Innistrad’s vampires gain ground against humanity, Sorin forges an angel to hold back the darkness in his absence. He names her Avacyn and tasks her with protecting the plane; through her, the Church of Avacyn is born, and most vampires brand Sorin a traitor to his race for it.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  4. Imprisoning Nahiri Sorin Nahiri

    Nahiri’s call for help against stirring Eldrazi on Zendikar is absorbed by the Helvault, Sorin’s prison vault, and never reaches him. When she confronts him over the oversight, Sorin shows no remorse and dismisses her; Nahiri threatens him physically, Avacyn intervenes, and Sorin ultimately banishes Nahiri into the Helvault — the betrayal that defines the rest of their relationship.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  5. Betrayed on Zendikar Sorin

    Millennia later, called back to Zendikar as the Eldrazi stir again, Sorin allies with the elf planeswalker Nissa Revane to reach the Eye of Ugin. At the last moment Nissa shatters the hedron containing the Eldrazi instead of reinforcing it, releasing the titans. Furious, Sorin washes his hands of her and the entire plane.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  6. Markov Manor in ruins Sorin Nahiri Olivia

    Sorin returns to Innistrad to find Markov Manor twisted into impossible shapes, its inhabitants embedded in the masonry — Nahiri’s declaration of vengeance. Shunned by most of his own kind, he seeks out the powerful vampire Olivia Voldaren to help rally the bloodline against the threat.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  7. Unmaking Avacyn Sorin Avacyn

    To secure Olivia’s help, Sorin agrees to destroy his own corrupted creation. He finds Avacyn — driven mad by Nahiri’s cryptoliths and the arrival of Emrakul — attacking Jace Beleren and Tamiyo, and tries to reason with her, offering to cleanse her mind. Avacyn refuses, blaming Sorin for making her flawed enough to be turned against the innocent. They fight through Thraben Cathedral; Sorin overpowers her and, with anguished regret, unmakes the angel he once called his daughter, one thousand years after her creation.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  8. War against Nahiri Sorin Nahiri Olivia

    Realizing too late that Avacyn’s destruction was exactly what Nahiri wanted — it summons Emrakul to the plane — Sorin and Olivia assemble a vampire army against her. Nahiri defeats him in single combat at Markov Manor and encases him in stone, leaving him helpless to stop Emrakul’s devastation. Olivia claims the ruined plane as her own and takes his sword.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  9. War of the Spark Sorin Nahiri

    Free of his stone prison, Sorin is drawn to Ravnica and finds Nahiri there too — even the multiversal threat of Nicol Bolas can’t stop their feud, and the two duel in single combat amid the battle. They eventually call a truce to fight Bolas’s Eternals alongside the Gatewatch, though their rivalry is assumed to resume once the war ends.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  10. Grief at Markov Manor Sorin

    Back on Innistrad, Sorin sequesters himself in his ruined ancestral home, mourning Avacyn and lamenting that every safeguard he built has fallen. When Arlinn Kord and the Gatewatch intrude seeking the Moonsilver Key, his grief turns to fury at the mention of his lost creation, and he attacks them; the angel Sigarda intervenes to buy the others time to escape.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

  11. Crashing the wedding Sorin Edgar

    When Olivia Voldaren charms his grandfather Edgar into agreeing to marry her, Sorin fights his way alone through the Voldaren estate’s guards to stop it, only to be dragged away in holy chains. After the ceremony is shattered by a freed Sigarda, Sorin confronts Edgar directly; the two come to blows, and in the depths of a blood pit Sorin draws on his family’s own sangromancy to heal and overpower his grandfather. Rather than killing him, Sorin shows mercy and orders him to leave.

    MTG Wiki: Sorin Markov

This timeline rests on one primary source, the MTG Wiki’s Sorin Markov page, itself built from citations to the original Wizards of the Coast story chapters.

Playing him as a commander

Only Sorin of House Markov can lead a Commander deck on his own. None of his seven solo planeswalker cards carry “can be your commander” text, so they’re Commander-legal to play inside a deck but can’t serve as the commander itself outside the Oathbreaker format — Sorin of House Markov starts as a legendary creature and transforms into a planeswalker, which is what makes him eligible. He leads 6,686 decks on EDHREC, built around lifegain (1,198 decks), lifedrain (183), vampires (167), and the Lurrus companion restriction (151). Sorin, Vengeful Bloodlord is by far the most-played Sorin planeswalker inside those decks, appearing in 43.9% of them. EDHREC counts decks of every pod size, not 1v1 specifically.

What could not be confirmed

  • The two Tarkir timelines. The MTG Wiki describes an “original timeline” where Sorin finds Ugin already dead, and a “current timeline” where Sorin’s visit (Sorin’s Restoration) awakens Ugin from a hedron cocoon. I did not attempt to reconcile how these two accounts relate; the timeline above follows the current-timeline version only.
  • Non-canon material. The wiki’s “Boom! comics” section and the Echoverse (an alternate-universe variant where Sorin is a werewolf) are explicitly separate from his main continuity and are omitted from the timeline above.
  • His current activity against New Phyrexia. The wiki says only that he and Arlinn Kord “worked together to prepare Innistrad’s defenses” and informed Geralf Cecani and Ludevic of the threat — I found no further detail on what came of it.
  • Exact wording of the story chapters. I read the MTG Wiki through a page-reading tool rather than the original Wizards story articles it cites, so check direct quotes against the source stories before relying on exact phrasing.
  • Prices and printings. Single lookups taken on one day; the printing list above may not be exhaustive.
  • Scryfall blocks direct automated requests, so oracle and flavor text came from a card-data tool, cross-checked against Scryfall pages loaded in a browser.
